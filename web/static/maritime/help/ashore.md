# Ashore

[Back to the handbook](index.md)

Walking down the gangway puts you in an ordinary room, in an ordinary town, and the ship is
still lying where you left her.

## Buying and selling

    browse
    buy a papaya
    buy rum punch from Ma Ravel
    sell
    market

`browse` is the one to start with: it lists who is selling here and what they have. `buy`
takes a thing, and optionally who from when two people are selling.

`market` is the other side of it — what this place wants and what it offers, which is how
you work out what is worth carrying where.

A street with nobody trading in it will tell you so. The verbs are on every room ashore
rather than only the ones with a counter, because a shop you have to find by trial is a
worse experience than an honest refusal.

## The map

Ashore, the interface stops being a chart and becomes a map of the town: the rooms, the
ways between them, and a mark on the one you are standing in.

**Click a room and you walk there.** The route is walked as ordinary movement, one step at
a time — nothing teleports, so a locked gate stops the walk exactly where it would stop
you, and you can watch it happen.

Every line on the map is one move. Where two rooms are joined but too far apart to draw an
honest line between them, the line is left out — the rooms are both still there, still
clickable, and clicking still routes over every exit that really exists.

Markers tell you what a room is worth stopping at: somewhere to trade, a way out of the
drawn area, and the room you are in.

## Leaving your ship

If you disembark and log out, your ship is laid up rather than left in the fairway. See
[harbours](harbours.md) for getting her back.
