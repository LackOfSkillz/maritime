# Her people

[Back to the handbook](index.md)

    crew

Her company: how many she carries, how many are still standing, what they are made of, how
they are bearing it, and what they hold against you.

## What they are

Three kinds, and they are not interchangeable:

| Rating | Working the ship | In a fight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seamen | Everything | Can fight at need |
| Oarsmen | Some | Less |
| Marines | Almost nothing | Everything |

A merchantman's whole company is seamen. Every marine is a man who eats, occupies space and
cannot reef a topsail — which is exactly why carrying any is a decision.

Quality runs from pressed men to a crack crew, and it multiplies both columns
independently. A crack crew of seamen is not a party of marines, and the game knows the
difference.

## What they cost you

**Everything aboard takes hands and takes time.** Setting sail, serving the guns, throwing
grapples, clearing them again — all of it is work, and how long it takes is how many hands
you have and how good they are. A ship that has lost half her people is slower at
everything, not merely weaker.

**They tire.** Pulling at `stretch out` spends them. Exhaustion sits on the ship rather
than on your character, because how tired a *person* gets belongs to the game this is
installed in and not to this.

**They can be frightened.** Casualties, being outgunned, and being on the losing end of a
boarding all tell. A shaken crew do not refuse — they are slower at everything and they lay
the guns worse, which is a far more useful failure than a flat "cannot".

**They can refuse.** Push them far enough and they will strike, or worse. That is not a
punishment for playing badly; it is the crew being people, and it is what makes their
condition worth watching.
