## Being taken in

    ports

What she can be told to make for, and why not for the rest. A harbour refuses for reasons:
she draws too much for the bar, there is no marked water in, it is beyond her range.

    make for Careenage

The whole passage, handed to the sailing master. He lays the course, stands on, takes the
marked channel, and brings her alongside at the end of it without another order from you.
It is the single most useful order in the game and it is also the one that will run you onto
something if you give it in water nobody has surveyed.

    belay

Takes the con back. Use it the moment you can see something he cannot.

## Doing it yourself

    dock

Comes alongside. She has to be:

- **close enough** — within reach of the berth, not merely in the harbour
- **slow enough** — a ship arriving at a quay at six knots is not docking
- **small enough** — the berth has a length, a beam and a depth, and she has to fit all
  three

The gangway that appears when she is made fast is a real way ashore. Walk down it.

    cast off

Lets go and takes the gangway away.

## While you are there

Ashore you can walk, trade and be robbed like anywhere else — see [ashore](ashore.md).

If you leave your ship and log out, she does not sit in the fairway forever. She is laid up
in ordinary, which is the harbour's way of saying somebody has taken her masts out and put
her somewhere tidy, and:

    maritime summon Kittiwake

brings her forward again when you come back. You can only summon a ship you own, and owning
one includes having taken her off somebody.
