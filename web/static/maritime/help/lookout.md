# The lookout

[Back to the handbook](index.md)

    lookout

What is in sight from where you are standing — and *where you are standing* is the point.
The masthead sees further than the deck, because the horizon is a function of how high your
eye is. Two metres above the water gives you about three miles. Twenty gives you nine.

Shipping and marks are reported apart, because they are different questions: one of them
might be hunting you and the other one tells you where you are.

    scan

The whole horizon, quarter by quarter.

    look se
    look port

One direction — a compass point or a quarter of the ship.

    watch north

A standing watch. You are told as things come and go rather than having to ask, which is
what you want when you are doing something else.

## What you can tell

You do not always get a name. At the edge of vision a ship is a shape; closer, a rig;
closer still, a ship you recognise. Weather and light take that away again — in fog or at
dusk you may know something is there and nothing else about it.

This matters for [the guns](guns.md): an order to hold fire for a *named* ship needs her
identified, and in fog you cannot identify anybody. An order to hold fire on an *arc* works
in any weather, and will fire at whatever crosses it, including your friends.

    target Marigold

Range, aspect, closure and which of your arcs bear on her. Aspect is what she is showing
you — bow-on, beam-on, or somewhere between — and it decides how big a target she is and
how much of her broadside can answer.
