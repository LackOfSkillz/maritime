# Oars

[Back to the handbook](index.md)

For craft that are pulled or paddled. Oars do not care where the wind is, which is their
whole point: a boat under oars can leave a harbour a square-rigger has to wait for.

    oars

What her looms are doing and how many hands are on them.

## The strokes

| Order | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| `easy` | A gentle stroke. Steerage way and no more. |
| `give way` | The working stroke. What you row at. |
| `stretch out` | Everything they have. Fast, and it costs them. |
| `hold water` | Oars in the water to stop her. |
| `paddle` | For craft that are paddled rather than rowed. |

## What it costs

Pulling tires people. `stretch out` is not a speed setting — it is an order to spend the
crew, and a crew spent is a crew who cannot do it again in an hour and who are closer to
refusing you. See [her people](crew.md).

Exhaustion sits on the ship, not on you: your own character does not get tired, because how
tired a *person* is belongs to the game this is installed in.

## Being run down

A ship that runs down your side will break the oars you have out — see
[ramming](ramming.md). Your looms break against her hull as they go, which hurts her a
little, but you are the one who loses the oars.

If you are under oars and something is driving at your beam, get them in or get out of the
way.
