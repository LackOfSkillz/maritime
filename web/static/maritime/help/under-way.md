# Under way

[Back to the handbook](index.md)

## Getting her off the quay

    cast off

Lets go the lines and takes the gangway away. You have to be aboard, and she has to be
alongside something to be let go from.

    weigh anchor

Brings the anchor home. A ship at anchor does not answer her helm until it is off the
ground.

## Steering

    helm 090

A course to steer, in degrees. Spoken to the helmsman and answered by him, and she comes
round at the rate her hull allows rather than instantly — a heavy ship takes a while, and
you can watch her do it.

Bearings are read as figures: `helm 090` is "helm, steer zero-nine-zero". Anything from
`000` to `359`.

    speed 6

For a vessel that is not under sail. Under sail, what she makes is a negotiation between
the wind and her canvas — see [sailing](sailing.md) — and ordering a speed will not get you
one the wind does not permit.

    allstop

Takes the way off her. She does not stop; she stops *driving*, and then carries her way
until the water takes it off.

## Bringing up

    drop anchor

Lets go and brings her up. She will lie to it and drift round with the tide and the wind,
which is not the same as being stopped.

    dock

Comes alongside a berth. She has to be close enough, slow enough, and the berth has to be
long enough and deep enough for her — see [harbours](harbours.md).

## What she will and will not do

She will not sail backwards. Order a reciprocal course instead.

She will not turn faster than her hull allows, and a big ship under a press of sail is not
a small one. If she is not answering, look at how much canvas she has set: too little and
she has no steerage way, too much and she is fighting you.

She will not sail through the ground. See [soundings](soundings.md), and read the leadsman.
